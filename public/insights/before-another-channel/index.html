Acquire

Before you add another channel

A new channel is easy to buy and hard to run. The useful question is whether the last one already has a clear next step.

Most marketing piles up. A social account here, a set of ads there, a newsletter someone meant to send. Each piece can be reasonable on its own. Together they often have no shared job.

Adding a channel feels like progress because it is visible. What you cannot see is whether the person who arrives knows what to do next, or whether anyone will follow up if they do.

Start from the gap, not the menu

Ask where growth is actually stuck. Is it that the right people cannot find you? That they find you and leave? Or that they buy once and disappear? Those are three different problems. A new channel only helps the first of them, and only if it is aimed.

If the offer is unclear, more traffic makes the confusion louder. If the landing page does not match the promise in the ad, the spend teaches you very little. If nobody owns the reply, the enquiry is a dead end with a nicer logo on it.

A smaller plan is usually the faster one

Pick one priority for the next stretch of work. Give it a channel that can actually reach the people you mean, a page or path that finishes the sentence, and a way to see whether anything changed. Then earn the next channel.

That is not a small ambition. It is how a marketing engine starts: one route that works, then another, joined on purpose.
