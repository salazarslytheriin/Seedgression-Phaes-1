The landing page has to finish the ad’s sentence

The click is not the result. It is the moment someone checks whether you meant what you just said.

An ad, a search result, or a post makes a promise in a very small space. The page that follows has one job: keep that promise, and show the next step without making the person hunt for it.

When the page talks about something else — a full company history, four offers, a menu of every service — the person has to start again. Many of them will not.

Match the message, then make one ask

Use the same words the person just responded to. If the ad was about a specific problem, the headline should still be about that problem. If the offer was a conversation, do not swap it for a twenty-field form.

One page, one action. A call, a short note, a booking, a purchase. Say what happens after they take it. People move faster when the next five minutes are obvious.

Read it as a stranger

Cover the logo and read only the headline, the first two sentences, and the button. Could a new customer tell what this is, who it is for, and what to do? If the answer needs a meeting to explain, the page is not finished.

Fix that before you buy a larger audience. Otherwise you pay to learn the same thing, more quickly.
