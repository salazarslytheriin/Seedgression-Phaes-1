Measure

What to do when the dashboard is loud

More charts do not make a decision easier. Start from the question the business actually needs answered.

A modern marketing account can report clicks, views, reach, frequency, scroll depth, and a dozen rates with confident colours. Some of those numbers are useful. Many of them are just nearby.

Vanity is not the number itself. It is treating a number as success when it never had to change a decision.

Three questions are enough to start

What did we do? What happened that the business can feel — enquiries, sales, return visits, pipeline, the cost of getting there? What will we change because of it?

If a report cannot help with the third question, it is a diary, not a tool. Keep the diary if you like. Do not let it run the meeting.

Be honest about what the data cannot see

Attribution is a model, not a witness. A person might see a post, ignore an ad, ask a friend, and then search your name. The dashboard will often give the credit to whichever click it noticed last.

Say that out loud. Then use the numbers you trust for the decision in front of you: which message people finish, which page they leave, which follow-up gets a reply. Small, clear measurements beat a wall of charts nobody acts on.
